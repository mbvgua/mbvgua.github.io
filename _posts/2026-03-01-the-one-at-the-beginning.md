Yes, this is the obligatory "pilot episode". The one that one must always be addressed at the beginning of a new adventure, be it a show, routine, project, career path e.t.c. The one in which the world points a spotlight on you and asks "_Who are you? And more so why should we care?_". And if we are being honest, I'm not sure I'd be able to answer that satisfactorily.

When we were younger, we did things just for the fun of it. Want to slide down that hill with no protective clothing whatsoever? _I wish you'd asked me sooner?_. Want to dance in front of you entire extended family? _Dude! I have been waiting for this moment my entire life. Having two left feet and only know that [one](https://youtu.be/3tqhl-g1OaQ?si=0ADfz0lrp_G-0SOj&t=277) dance move from ages ago will only be a plus_ Want to take a swim, even though it freezing outside? _Why not? Infact, don't bother with the fact that I don't know how to swim, and probably won't for the rest of my life ;)_. It was all fun and games through and through.

In time, we grew older and realized that Superman was not real and everything else just went downhill from here. We couldn't do things "just because" anymore. Your reasons needed reasons[^1]. They had to be quantifiable. The cons had to outweigh the cons. Profits had to made. Lives had to be changed for the net positive. Ergo why say I wouldn't be able to answer _that_ question satisfactorily. If I was to think about it, the time I have invested into building this blog so far is pretty significant. The amount of time spent on fine tuning the ~~nonsense~~ details is laughable. And that is not including all the material to be written by future me. Time is precious, and time spent by a developer on a project is everything. It reaches to a point where its clear there are no serious ROI in further time spent(why do you think we have so many ~~dead projects~~ side projects).

But looking atthe flipside, there are areas that I might a major positive massgain. Saymy eleoquece, might improveif Iplay mycards right. my clarity ofthoughts might also see a spike if iensure im consisitent enough. helpingthe community is another. Honestly, most articles that actually did helpmefigure sth I was stuck on seldomcame from the aesthetically pleasing medium and dev.to blogposts. theywere from a site that looked straight from the 90's, terrible deign butthe content was just top notch.

If the spotlight did shine on me and I had to announce to everyone why I decided to venture into this unfamiliar and perilous territory that would bring about new, <small>_cough! wonderful cough!_</small>, experiences instead of sticking to the all too familiar route I've grown all so accustomed to, then I'd have to day "just because" :)

For a good while now, I have been thinking of starting a blog, a tech blog if you will. For anyone brought up around the same culture as me, I am sure the first thing on youre mind is; _Well, that's great and all, but more importantly, what are you're 3 whys?_ [^1]. And I'm glad you asked!

The first one is a bit intrinsic, which is the best kind. I would like to get better at this 'tech stuff'. I think it goes without saying that writing helps solidify concepts and ideas that you have learnt, moving them from [the short-term memory section to the long-term memory](https://scientistsinthemaking.com/cognitive-science-how-to-turn-information-into-long-term-memory/). In tech, you're constantly learning new concepts or relearning something that you have done a multitude of times before simply because you did not grasp its fundamental workings[^3]. Writing these concepts down will no doubt help me understand the what and why's better!

The second one is a cool middleground. I've always loved writing ever since I was a kid. The idea of thinking up wild radomn ideas, putting them down on paper and having another human being liking(or even relating) to your thoughts is pretty amazing. Building the collection of 'dictionary words' needed to be eloquent and express yourself well with nothing being lost in translation has always fascinated me. I think this is what drew me to programming, it a rather modernized means of self expression, but with the medium being 1's and 0's.

Those are my 3 why's :] Also, for anyone who might be curious, I actually did try using the most popular platforms circa '26, namely [dev.to](https://dev.to/) and [medium](https://medium.com/). They were nice, but at this point I'm too far gone in the rabbit hole of software sovereighnity and freedom to _not_ choose the path the might eventually lead me to self-hosting my own website, someday atleast, so I better get to it!
