## Introduction

SCOM(Service for Christ Outreach Ministry) is a home that caters to differently-abled children. Like most goodwill organisations, they run primarily on the owners funds, but could usually benefit greatly from donations and goodwill. This led me to a lighbulb moment; helping them to cultivate an online presence would make them known to a larger audience,in turn attracting well wishers from both near and far. As such I quickly got to work.

Initially it was meant to be a static site, with minimal functionality, and I formed this preconceived notion that it would me a week at most to have it up and running, but man was I wrong. Having taken a little bit of time off of building frontend applications, my recall of basic things was hazy at best. I struggled doing even the most basic of things [^1]. Also, thanks to **_feature creep_** I ended up doing some things I had not even thought of, and this is why I opted to built the application using a backend framework(fastAPI) with the [MVC pattern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model%E2%80%93view%E2%80%93controller). However, it all worked out in the end, taking a little bit more time than I had anticipated at first, but I did eventually build the final [website](https://scomhome.vercel.app). In this article, I'll focus on some of the sections whose implementation I found <sup>~~difficult~~</sup> interesting.

## Making the forms work

Forms are the standard way in which users communicate with the site owners over HTTP. Ofcourse, there are more ways for data to be transferred,but forms and websockets were my main options and I went with the former. The site needed two main forms; the main [contact us form](https://scomhome.vercel.app/contact-us), which serves as the main data entrypoint of the application, and another [get involved form](https://scomhome.vercel.app/get-involved) to serve volunteers and well-wishers.
