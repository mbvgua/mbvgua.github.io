For a good while now, I have been thinking of starting a blog, a tech blog if you will. For anyone brought up around the same culture as me, I am sure the first thing on youre mind is; *Well, that's great and all, but more importantly, what are you're 3 whys?* [^1]. And I'm glad you asked!

The first one is a bit intrinsic, which is the best kind. I would like to get better at this 'tech stuff'. I think it goes without saying that writing helps solidify concepts and ideas that you have learnt, moving them from [the short-term memory section to the long-term memory](https://scientistsinthemaking.com/cognitive-science-how-to-turn-information-into-long-term-memory/). In tech, you're constantly learning new concepts or relearning something that you have done a multitude of times before simply because you did not grasp its fundamental workings[^3]. Writing these concepts down will no doubt help me understand the what and why's better!

The second one is a cool middleground. I've always loved writing ever since I was a kid. The idea of thinking up wild radomn ideas, putting them down on paper and having another human being liking(or even relating) to your thoughts is pretty amazing. Building the collection of 'dictionary words' needed to be eloquent and express yourself well with nothing being lost in translation has always fascinated me. I think this is what drew me to programming, it a rather modernized means of self expression, but with the medium being 1's and 0's.

Last but not least is for the community! If you were technical enough to view this repository's source code, then you might have caught a glimpse of the LICENSE, which is of the free and libre kind[^4]. I love the (fl)oss community as it is what has nurtured me in my career. Arguably, at this point I am not yet able to build an entire kernel like Torvalds or put together the needed tools needed to make it work on a desktop like Stallman(*massive emphasis on the yet*), but I hope this blog will serve as a helpful guide to wanderers of this space. I look forward to writing some interesting pieces that may help people discover essential tools for their arsenal and also work on fun projects.

Those are my 3 why's :] Also, for anyone who might be curious, I actually did try using the most popular platforms circa '26, namely [dev.to](https://dev.to/) and [medium](https://medium.com/). They were nice, but at this point I'm too far gone in the rabbit hole of software sovereighnity and freedom to *not* choose the path the might eventually lead me to self-hosting my own website, someday atleast, so I better get to it!

See you in the next one...
